New assets default to **After-Tax** and liabilities to **None**.

## Built-in tax types

| Tax type | Meaning | Examples |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **After-Tax** | Taxed yearly on interest or gains | Checking, Savings, Brokerage |
| **Tax-Deferred** | Tax break now, taxed on withdrawal | Traditional IRA, 401(k) |
| **Roth** | Taxed now, tax-free withdrawals | Roth IRA |
| **Tax-Advantaged** | Special benefits for health or education | HSA, 529 |
| **Mixed** | More than one type in one account | 401(k) with Roth and traditional |
| **None** | No tax treatment | Mortgages, loans, credit cards |

## Custom tax types

Create one from the **Tax Status** picker or **Settings → Custom Accounts → Tax Types**. A type can't be deleted while an account uses it.

## Tax Categories card

The **Tax Categories** [Dashboard card](/help/dashboard/customize-dashboard/) shows how your money splits across tax types in two bars:

- **Current:** today's balances
- **Projected:** your balances in the year the latest [Retirement Age](/help/family/family-profiles/#retirement-age-and-savings-age) is reached

Switch between **%** and amounts at the top. Accounts set to **None**, like mortgages and loans, aren't included.

This makes it easy to see your tax diversification. Money in Roth, tax-deferred, and after-tax accounts is taxed differently when you withdraw it. A healthy mix gives you more flexibility to manage taxes in retirement, and the projected bar shows whether today's contributions are moving you toward that mix.
