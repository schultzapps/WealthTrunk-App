Each plan below says which [projection type](/help/scenarios/linked-vs-manual/) fits best. For anything about homes, see [Homes and moves](/help/scenarios/homes-and-moves/).

:::tip
Use **Duplicate** to try variations. Keep your base plan, copy it, change one thing, and compare the **ending value** and **Alerts**. Sort scenarios by **Ending Value** to rank them.
:::

## Retire at a certain age

**Use Linked.**

1. Create a scenario for yourself, or **Joint** for you and a partner.
2. Open the **umbrella** button and set **Retirement Age** and **Life Expectancy**.
3. On **Spending**, check or add **Living Expenses**, your monthly spending in retirement.
4. On **Income**, add what arrives in retirement: **Government Pension** at the age benefits begin, a **Workplace Pension**, or **Part-Time Work**.
5. In **Edit**, set **Return During Drawdown** and **Tax on Withdrawals**.
6. Read the chart and **Alerts**. If money runs out, the year is shown.

Your account contributions continue until retirement automatically, so don't add your salary.

## Retire earlier, or work longer

**Use Linked.** Duplicate your scenario and change only the **Retirement Age**. Compare the two ending values. A few years of extra saving and fewer years of spending usually matters more than a small change in return.

If retiring early, add **Health Care** spending that starts at retirement and **Ends** at **At an Age…** when other coverage starts, so the gap is covered.

## Phase into retirement

**Use Linked.** Add **Part-Time Work** income starting at **Retires** and ending **At an Age…** (like 67), and spending as usual.

## Spending that changes in retirement

**Use Linked** (or Manual). Rather than one **Living Expenses** amount, split it. Keep **Living Expenses** for your base, then add **Travel** (yearly) that starts at retirement and ends **At an Age…** when you'd slow down, and a **Health Care** that gets bigger later. Set **Annual Change** if it rises faster than prices.

## Sabbatical or career break

**Use Manual.** A sabbatical pauses your income for a stretch, then it resumes. Contributions in Linked can't pause on a date, so Manual shows it accurately.

1. **Edit → Projection Type → Manual**.
2. On **Income**, add your **Salary**. Set it to **End** **On a Date…** when the break begins.
3. Add a second **Salary** that **Starts** **On a Date…** when you return (perhaps at a new amount) and **Ends** at **Retires**.
4. On **Spending**, add today's monthly spending, **Starts** **Now** and ending at retirement. Add retirement spending starting at **Retires**.
5. Optionally add one-time travel or education costs for the break.

Duplicate the scenario and pause the break to compare against working through.

## Going part-time or changing jobs

**Use Manual.** End your **Salary** on the date you change, then add a new salary or **Part-Time Work** starting that date at the new amount. Use **Annual Change** if raises are expected.

## Inheritance or windfall

**Use Linked** (Manual works too).

1. On **Income**, add **Inheritance**.
2. Enter the **Amount**. Its frequency is **Once**.
3. Set **Starts** to **On a Date…** or **At an Age…**, depending on whether you know when.

The money is added to your savings and grows with them. If you're unsure of the date, duplicate the scenario and test different years, or leave the inheritance out and see if the plan holds without it. If it's being spent, such as paying off a home, add that too: use **Pay Off Early** on the mortgage in Linked.

## Gifts, weddings, and other big one-time costs

**Use Linked** (or Manual). Add a **Large Purchase** or **Custom** spending set to **Once** on the date. For something that repeats each year, like gifts to family, use **Yearly**.

## Child's college

**Use Either.**

- **In your plan:** add **Education** spending, **Yearly**, **Starts At an Age…** a child's age (choose the child's profile) and **Ends** four or more years later.
- **For the child:** create a scenario for the **dependent**. They plan their savings years, like a 529 for **University**.

## Buy a car

**Use Either.** For cash, add **Vehicle Purchase** (**Once**). If financed, add the monthly payment as **Spending** that ends when the loan does, and set **Annual Change** to the negative of inflation so the payment stays level. An existing car loan is already on your account, so only add payments for a new one.

## Pay off debt faster

**Use Linked.** Tap the debt and raise **Monthly Payment**, or use **Pay Off Early** to clear it on a date. See [Homes and moves](/help/scenarios/homes-and-moves/#change-an-account-in-a-scenario). Spending that depends on the payoff, like redirecting the payment to savings, can use **Debt Paid Off** as its start.

## Rental or side income

**Use Either.** Add **Rental Income** or a **Custom** income. In Linked this is for income no account saves. In Manual, add the costs too.

## Higher or lower expenses

**Use Either.** Change the **Amount** of **Living Expenses**, or add a **Custom** spending that starts when your life changes: a child, a move, caring for a parent.

## Longer life or a safety margin

**Use Linked.** Raise **Life Expectancy** to 95 or 100 and see whether the plan still works. Lower **Return During Drawdown** and raise **Inflation Rate** to stress-test.

## Already retired

Set **Retirement Age** to **Retired**. The projection runs 40 years, and your accounts' contributions continue to apply. Add your spending and any pension or Social Security.

## Tips for reliable results

- **Match your accounts.** Keep [balances up to date](/help/accounts/recording-balances/). Scenarios use your live balances.
- **Don't double count.** In Linked, skip your salary and today's spending. In Manual, include them.
- **Choose realistic returns.** A conservative return is safer than the best case. Check the **Alerts**.
- **Use the year list.** Tap the chart and scan the **Cash Flow** for years that look wrong.
- **Revisit.** Update scenarios when your life or the market changes.
