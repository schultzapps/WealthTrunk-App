Every scenario has a **Projection Type**: **Linked** or **Manual**. Change it any time in **Edit → Projection Type**, or tap **Learn More...** there to read about both in the app.

## The difference

| | **Linked** | **Manual** |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **How you save before retirement** | Each account adds its own **Monthly Contribution** | Income minus spending. What's left each month is saved/invested. |
| **How money grows** | Each account at its own **Expected Return** | All included accounts pooled into one net worth, growing at one return |
| **Debts** | Pay down with their own **Monthly Payment** and can be timed to a payoff | Not tracked. Enter loan payments as spending. |
| **Income** | Only for money no account saves: a pension, rental, or one-time windfall | Everything: salary, pension, and the rest |
| **Spending** | Usually just retirement spending | Today's spending and retirement spending, including loan payments |
| **Buying or selling a home** | The home, its mortgage, price, and costs are all modeled | Only costs and mortgage payments apply. The home's own growth isn't tracked. |
| **Per-account changes** | Tap an account to change its return, contribution, or payment in this scenario only | Not used, apart from leaving accounts out |
| **Chart** | Net Worth, Assets, Liabilities, or Liquid Assets | Net Worth only |

## Linked: start here

Linked uses the contributions and payments you already set up on your accounts, so it takes the least work. Use it when:

- You're **saving the way you do today** and want to see when you can retire
- You want to compare **retirement ages**, spending levels, or returns
- You're adding **one-time events**: an inheritance, a big purchase, selling a home
- You want to **buy, sell, or move** and see the home and mortgage in your net worth
- You want to **change an account**, like a higher contribution, a lower loan rate after a refinance, or paying off a debt early

Because your accounts already save for you, don't add your salary in Linked. It would be counted twice. Add income only for money that doesn't go into an account.

Likewise, you'll usually add spending only for retirement. Today's spending is already reflected in what you contribute.

## Manual: when income and spending change

Manual builds the plan from cashflows instead of contributions. Use it when your **income or spending will change in ways account contributions can't show**:

- A **sabbatical** or career break, where salary stops and then resumes
- **Going part-time** or switching to a lower-paying job
- **Moving** somewhere with a very different cost of living
- **Having a child**, where spending rises for years
- Retiring early and bridging with **spending and income** you control

Everything you add is explicit: pause or change any income or spending to see the effect. The tradeoff is more to enter. Add your salary, your spending today, and your spending in retirement.

Manual uses one return for your whole net worth. It starts at your accounts' balance-weighted average (the Edit screen shows it) and you can change it.

## Choosing quickly

| You want to model | Use |
| --- | --- |
| Retiring at a certain age | **Linked** |
| Retiring earlier or later | **Linked** |
| An inheritance or windfall | **Linked** |
| Buying, selling, or downsizing a home | **Linked** |
| Paying off a mortgage early or refinancing | **Linked** |
| A sabbatical or career break | **Manual** |
| Part-time work before full retirement | **Manual** |
| A big change in monthly spending | **Manual** |
| A child's college costs | Either |

When unsure, use Linked, then switch to Manual if you can't express your plan.

## Switching types

You can switch back and forth. Your income, spending, and ages stay. Some things behave differently:

- Moving to **Manual**, anything timed to a debt's payoff becomes the year the debt would be paid off, since debts aren't tracked there.
- In Manual, your accounts only set the **starting net worth**. Their contributions and payments aren't used.
- If you switch from Linked to Manual, add your salary, which Linked didn't need.

:::tip
Duplicate a scenario, switch the copy's type, and compare the two results. It's a good check that your plan holds up either way.
:::
